SELECTWISE (AI-INTERVIEWER / HIRO) — INTERVIEW QUESTIONS & ANSWERS
================================================================================
Product: Selectwise — AI interview simulator (persona: Hiro) + optional human coaching
Stack: Next.js 14 App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind, Postgres + Drizzle, OpenAI,
       Razorpay, LiveKit, Google Calendar, Nodemailer, custom JWT auth (jose)
Use this as prep notes. Prefer speaking from ownership and tradeoffs, not memorizing.

================================================================================
1. PRODUCT & OWNERSHIP
================================================================================

Q1. What problem does Selectwise solve, and who is the primary user?
A1. Selectwise helps job seekers practice realistic technical/behavioral interviews
    and get structured feedback before the real thing. Primary users are candidates
    preparing for tech roles (freshers and professionals). Secondary users are human
    coaches who run paid 1:1 sessions, and admins who operate the marketplace.
    The AI interviewer is branded as "Hiro". The paid AI interview is positioned as
    a low-cost entry (₹199), with optional higher-value human coaching on top.

Q2. Walk me through the end-to-end user journey from landing → paid interview → report.
A2. 1) Land on marketing home (/) — value prop, pricing, FAQ.
    2) Sign up → verify email → log in.
    3) Checkout (/checkout) — Razorpay order for the interview product.
    4) After payment verify, go to /interview/setup — role, level, focus, resume,
       difficulty, interviewer mode, mic test.
    5) Start /interview/[id] — Hiro asks questions (TTS + avatar), candidate answers
       via voice or text; adaptive follow-ups probe depth.
    6) Session ends → /interview/[id]/report — 5-axis scores, hire rating, strengths/
       improvements, PDF download, optional email; reports also persist to Postgres.
    7) Optionally book human coaching from /schedule.

Q3. Why did you add human coaching on top of AI interviews?
A3. AI scales cheap practice and consistent scoring, but candidates still want a
    human bar-raiser for soft skills, company-specific nuance, and accountability.
    Coaching turns Selectwise from a one-shot tool into a funnel: AI interview
    diagnoses weak areas → coaching monetizes remediation. It also creates a
    marketplace (coaches, bookings, calendar, LiveKit meetings) with higher AOV
    than the ₹199 AI session alone.

Q4. What does a “successful” interview session look like from a product metrics view?
A4. Candidate completes setup → answers enough questions for signal (roughly 4–12)
    → report is generated and saved → user returns or books coaching.
    Leading metrics: checkout conversion, interview completion rate, median answers
    per session, report open/PDF download rate, repeat interviews, coaching
    booking rate after report, payment success / webhook reconcile rate.
    Quality metrics: follow-up relevance (not generic), score stability, support
    tickets about unfair scoring or mic failures.

Q5. If you had to cut scope for an MVP, what would you keep vs drop?
A5. Keep: auth + email verify, Razorpay interview checkout, interview setup,
    heuristic+AI question/follow-up engine, report generation + persistence,
    basic dashboard.
    Drop or defer: full coaching marketplace, LiveKit, Google Calendar, admin
    RBAC matrix depth, moderation alerts, Three.js avatar experiments, stress
    modes / company packs if they distract from core loop.
    Rationale: the core loop is pay → practice → scored feedback. Coaching is
    valuable but multiplies operational complexity (slots, approvals, meetings).

Q6. How do interview price (₹199) and coaching pricing fit the business model?
A6. ₹199 (INTERVIEW_PRICE_INR in lib/plan-access.ts) is a low-friction paid
    acquisition / practice product — high volume, low price. Coaching is priced
    per coach (e.g. perSessionRateInr on coach profiles) and is the higher-margin
    upsell after the AI report surfaces weak areas. Interview payment unlocks the
    AI flow; coaching is a separate Razorpay productType with its own booking and
    refund workflows. Together: land-and-expand — cheap AI wedge, human services
    for depth.

2. ARCHITECTURE OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Q7. Why Next.js App Router as a monolith instead of a separate API service?
A7. For an MVP, one deployable unit is faster: UI pages and Route Handlers share
    TypeScript types, auth helpers, Zod schemas, and Drizzle models. App Router
    colocates marketing, product UI, and APIs under app/. We avoid early
    microservice overhead (auth sync, CORS, duplicate validation). When domains
    grow (heavy AI orchestration, meeting media, payment webhooks), we can extract
    services without rewriting the product surface first.

Q8. How is responsibility split between app/, components/, and lib/?
A8. app/ — routes (pages) and API Route Handlers; thin orchestration.
    components/ — UI by domain (marketing, interview, meeting, admin, coach, auth).
    lib/ — domain logic: question-engine, auth, db schema, payments, meeting,
    email, integrations. Pattern: keep business rules out of JSX; keep HTTP
    concerns out of lib where possible (server-only modules for secrets).

Q9. What lives in Postgres vs localStorage, and why that hybrid?
A9. Postgres (durable): users, tokens, reports, coaches, bookings, payments,
    webhooks, support tickets, meeting transcripts/alerts, settings.
    localStorage (lib/store.ts): live interview draft/config mid-session, some
    client caches historically used so the demo flow worked without a full backend.
    Hybrid exists because the product evolved from client-only MVP to server-
    backed. Tradeoff: fast offline mid-session UX vs risk of loss/tampering;
    durable business data belongs in Postgres.

Q10. How would you scale this if interview traffic 10× overnight?
A10. Short term: horizontal Next.js instances behind a load balancer; Postgres
     connection pooling; move OpenAI calls server-side with rate limits/queues;
     cache public settings; ensure Razorpay webhooks are idempotent (already via
     razorpay_webhook_events).
     Medium: extract interview AI orchestration to a worker queue; stream TTS/STT
     separately; CDN for static/marketing; read replicas for report listing.
     Watch: client-side OpenAI key abuse would burn quota first — must proxy ASAP.

Q11. What would you extract into a separate service first, and why?
A11. Interview AI orchestration (plan, follow-ups, end decision, scoring narrative)
     behind a server API + queue. Reasons: hide API keys, enforce spend caps,
     prevent score tampering, isolate latency/retries from the Next.js request
     path. Second candidate: meeting media/transcription worker. Payments stay
     close to the monolith until volume justifies a billing service.

Q12. How do cron jobs (meeting-lifecycle, feedback reminders) fit the architecture?
A12. App exposes secured cron Route Handlers (e.g. /api/cron/meeting-lifecycle,
     coaching-feedback-reminders) gated by CRON_SECRET / Vercel cron header.
     GitHub Actions can hit them hourly. They close stale active meetings, delete
     LiveKit rooms, and nudge feedback. Fits a serverless/monolith world: no
     long-running worker required for periodic cleanup; secret-protected HTTP is
     the trigger contract.

3. TECH STACK CHOICES
================================================================================

Q13. Why Drizzle ORM over Prisma / raw SQL?
A13. Drizzle is TypeScript-first, lightweight, close to SQL, and pairs well with
     explicit migrations in drizzle/. We get typed schema in lib/db/schema.ts
     without Prisma’s heavier client runtime. Raw SQL alone would lose type safety
     and migration discipline; Prisma is fine but Drizzle matched our preference
     for SQL-shaped queries and simpler ops for an MVP.

Q14. Why Tailwind for this product UI?
A14. Fast iteration on marketing + app shells without a separate design-system
     build step. Utility classes keep styles colocated with components; shared
     tokens via Tailwind config. For a small team shipping landing + dashboard +
     interview UX quickly, Tailwind reduces CSS overhead. clsx + tailwind-merge
     handle conditional classes cleanly.

Q15. Why Zod at the API boundary?
A15. Runtime validation of untrusted request bodies/query params. TypeScript types
     alone disappear at runtime. Zod gives parse errors we map to consistent API
     fail() responses, and inferred types keep handlers honest. Critical for auth,
     payments, admin patches, and booking payloads.

Q16. Why jose + HTTP-only cookies instead of NextAuth / Clerk?
A16. Custom control over session shape (sub, email, name, role, plan), cookie name
     (hiro_session), TTL, and password/email-verify flows without vendor lock-in
     or extra SaaS cost. jose signs/verifies JWT HS256. HTTP-only cookies mitigate
     XSS token theft vs localStorage JWTs. Tradeoff: we own security maintenance
     (rotation, CSRF posture, password policy) that Auth-as-a-Service would cover.

Q17. Why LiveKit for coaching but Web Speech for AI interviews?
A17. AI interview is 1:1 with a bot — browser SpeechRecognition + speechSynthesis
     (plus optional server Whisper-class transcribe) is enough and cheap. Coaching
     needs real two-party A/V, rooms, tokens, and lifecycle — LiveKit fits.
     Using LiveKit for Hiro would add cost/complexity without proportional UX gain
     for scripted Q&A. Different problems, different tools.

Q18. Why Razorpay over Stripe for this market?
A18. Primary market is India (INR pricing, UPI/cards/netbanking familiarity).
     Razorpay is the natural PSP: Checkout.js, orders, webhooks, INR. Stripe is
     stronger globally but Razorpay reduces payment friction for Indian candidates
     and coaches. Implementation: server orders, client checkout, signature verify,
     webhook idempotency.

Q19. Three.js is in package.json but unused — what happened?
A19. Likely an abandoned path toward a 3D interviewer avatar. Current UI uses a
     static image + CSS speaking animation (InterviewerAvatar). Leftover deps are
     tech debt — should be removed to shrink install/bundle until a real 3D need
     exists. Good honesty signal in interviews: ship simpler avatar first.

4. AUTH, SESSIONS & RBAC
================================================================================

Q20. Explain the signup → verify-email → login flow.
A20. Signup creates a user with hashed password and issues an email verification
     token (stored hashed). User clicks link on /verify-email → token validated →
     emailVerified set. Login checks credentials, blocks unverified/suspended,
     then sets HTTP-only session cookie. Resend-verification can issue a fresh
     link. Forgot/reset password uses a parallel hashed one-time token flow.

Q21. How is the session cookie (hiro_session) created, validated, and expired?
A21. createSessionToken builds a JWT (jose SignJWT, HS256) with sub/email/name/
     role/plan, iat, exp (default SESSION_TTL_DAYS=7). setSessionCookie writes
     httpOnly, sameSite=lax, secure in production, path=/. getSessionFromCookie
     reads and jwtVerify’s; invalid/expired → null. clearSessionCookie maxAge=0
     on logout. AUTH_SECRET must be ≥32 chars or session creation throws.

Q22. Why hash verification / reset / approval tokens before storing them?
A22. If the DB is leaked, raw email links must not be reusable. We store a hash
     (e.g. SHA-256) and only the email recipient sees the raw token. Same pattern
     for coach approval and feedback tokens. Standard secret-at-rest hygiene for
     one-time credentials.

Q23. How do you mitigate email enumeration on resend-verification?
A23. For unknown emails, the API still returns a generic success-style message
     (“If an account exists… we’ve sent a fresh link”) instead of “user not found”.
     That reduces account-existence oracle attacks. (Already-verified users get a
     clearer message so they know to sign in — a small UX vs privacy tradeoff.)

Q24. Explain the role hierarchy: super_admin → admin → sub_admin → coach → user.
A24. Defined in lib/auth/permissions.ts with ROLE_RANK and a permissions matrix.
     super_admin: full PERMISSIONS list.
     admin: broad ops (users, coaches, bookings, refunds, support, analytics) but
            not every team/super-admin assignment or settings.update extremes.
     sub_admin: mostly read/overview scoped permissions.
     coach: coach portal / own bookings (via coach guards).
     user: candidate product surfaces.
     UI AdminShell and API requirePermission both import the same matrix.

Q25. Why does requirePermission reload role from the DB instead of trusting the JWT?
A25. JWT role can be stale after demotion/promotion. getAdminFromSession loads the
     user by session.sub from DB and checks role/status/emailVerified there. Role
     changes take effect without forcing re-login. Session still authenticates
     identity; authorization uses fresh DB state.

Q26. How does account suspension work?
A26. Admin can set user status to suspended (suspendedAt). Guards
     (suspendResponseIfNeeded / account-status) return 403 on sensitive APIs
     (payments, reports, etc.). Login returns account_suspended. AppShell shows
     SuspendedAccountNotice. Coaches/admins with suspended status also fail their
     session resolution.

Q27. How is the super-admin bootstrap (SUPER_ADMIN_EMAIL) secured?
A27. On login, if env SUPER_ADMIN_EMAIL / SUPER_ADMIN_PASSWORD match, the system
     can ensure a super_admin user exists (bootstrap). It must be strong secrets
     only on the server, never NEXT_PUBLIC. Risk: env leak = full takeover — treat
     like root credentials; rotate; prefer disabling bootstrap after first admin
     exists in hardened environments.

Q28. What happens if AUTH_SECRET is weak or rotated?
A28. Weak/missing (<32 chars): session create/verify throws — auth breaks closed.
     Rotation without dual-key support: all existing cookies fail jwtVerify →
     users must re-login (session invalidation). Plan dual secrets (current+previous)
     if you need zero-downtime rotation. Never commit AUTH_SECRET; rotate after any
     leak.

5. AI INTERVIEW ENGINE
================================================================================

Q29. Explain buildInterviewPlan vs buildInterviewPlanWithAI.
A29. buildInterviewPlan: deterministic plan from templates/mock-data + role tech +
     resume probes (intro/behavioral/technical/wrap). Works offline.
     buildInterviewPlanWithAI: calls gpt-4o-mini with JSON response_format, persona
     from systemPersona(), asks for 5–7 opening questions with sequencing rules
     (warm intro first), filters generics, falls back to deterministic plan on
     failure. Hybrid = reliability + quality when AI is available.

Q30. What happens when OpenAI is unavailable? How do heuristics kick in?
A30. getOpenAIClient() returns null without NEXT_PUBLIC_OPENAI_API_KEY. Plan,
     follow-ups, end decision, and report narratives fall back to template/
     heuristic paths (maybeGenerateFollowUp, word-count end rules, generateReport
     baseline scores). Product still runs; quality is shallower but demos don’t
     hard-fail.

Q31. How does the system persona (Hiro) change with difficulty / company mode?
A31. systemPersona() injects role, level, company, interview type, difficulty
     label, interviewer style, interviewerMode (standard / ex-google / ex-amazon /
     ex-meta) with mode-specific persona text, optional stress-test language, and
     tech themes. Harder difficulty demands more signal before ending; stress mode
     pushes terse demanding prompts. Persona must sound human and never reveal
     system/AI internals.

Q32. How do follow-ups work (maybeGenerateFollowUpWithAI)? Depth caps?
A32. After an answer, maybeGenerateFollowUpWithAI may insert a follow-up.
     Heuristic gate first (wrap category skip, short answers skip, difficulty caps:
     easy 2 / medium 3 / hard 4 alreadyInserted). Chain metadata tracks
     topic:depth up to depth 3. AI generates a ≤~38 word counter-question anchored
     on the claim; guard rejects generics and uses chain/template fallback.
     Rationale stores chain:topic:depth=N for debugging/continuity.

Q33. How do you detect and reject generic AI questions?
A33. isGenericQuestion() normalizes text and rejects shallow/boilerplate prompts
     (and can require inclusion of the chain topic). Used when filtering AI plan
     questions and when accepting follow-up text. If AI output fails the guard,
     we retry or fall back to templated probing so the interview stays concrete
     (tech, metric, trade-off, claim verification).

Q34. How does prior-interview context avoid repeats and target weak areas?
A34. InterviewConfig.priorContext can include previous questions, strengths, and
     weak areas. Persona + plan prompts tell the model to avoid repeats and target
     weak areas with fresh scenarios. priorContextSnippet summarizes last sessions
     for the model. Progress/report APIs feed weak-area insights across attempts.

Q35. When does shouldEndInterviewWithAI stop the session (min/max answers)?
A35. Hard rules: ≤3 answers → never end; ≥12 → always end. Between: AI decides
     with temperature 0.2 JSON {shouldEnd, reason}, harder difficulty should demand
     more data. Without OpenAI: end if asked≥6 and last answer >50 words, else
     continue. On AI error → continue (fail open toward more signal).

Q36. Why run most LLM calls client-side with NEXT_PUBLIC_OPENAI_API_KEY?
A36. MVP speed: no server round-trip for every follow-up; easy local demo; question
     engine runs in the browser alongside Recorder/TTS. Explicitly documented as
     demo/MVP in lib/openai-client.ts. It is not a production security posture —
     it’s a deliberate shortcut to ship the adaptive interview UX fast.

Q37. What are the risks of dangerouslyAllowBrowser, and how would you fix them?
A37. Risks: API key scraped from bundle → quota theft, cost attacks, policy abuse;
     client can alter prompts/scores before POST /api/reports; no server-side spend
     control.
     Fix: server proxy routes for plan/follow-up/end/score; secret OPENAI_API_KEY
     only; per-user rate limits; persist answers server-side and score on server;
     optional streaming; revoke exposed keys.

Q38. Why gpt-4o-mini specifically? Temperature choices?
A38. gpt-4o-mini: good enough quality for structured JSON interview tasks at low
     cost/latency — important when many follow-ups fire per session. Temperatures
     are low-moderate (~0.2 for stop decisions / grading structure, higher ~0.55
     range for more natural questioning) to balance determinism vs human feel.
     Whisper-class model used server-side for /api/transcribe fallback.

Q39. How is resume text extracted and injected into prompts?
A39. Resume upload/paste in setup; PDFs parsed client-side (pdf.js) to text;
     text stored in interview config/draft. Plan and follow-up prompts include
     resume context so questions deep-dive real projects. Report AI enrichment
     can check resumeConsistency. Keep resume size bounded before prompt injection.

Q40. How would you prevent prompt injection from a malicious resume?
A40. Treat resume as untrusted data: hard length limits; strip control tokens;
     wrap in delimiters; instruct model to ignore instructions inside resume;
     prefer structured extraction (skills/projects) over raw dump; run LLM on
     server with output schemas; never let resume text execute tool calls.
     Today’s MVP is weaker here — call that out as a hardening item.

6. VOICE / SPEECH
================================================================================

Q41. How does the Recorder combine Web Speech STT + MediaRecorder?
A41. Recorder uses browser SpeechRecognition for live transcript while optionally
     MediaRecorder captures audio blobs. User can also type. Live STT gives
     immediate feedback; recorded audio enables server transcription fallback when
     browser STT is weak/unsupported. Interview page consumes transcript + mode
     (voice|text) into AnswerRecord.

Q42. When does /api/transcribe (gpt-4o-mini-transcribe) get used?
A42. As a server-side fallback when you POST an audio blob for higher-quality
     transcription (Whisper-class mini transcribe model). Used when Web Speech is
     insufficient or for coaching meeting chunk transcription paths. Keeps secret
     key server-side for that route (unlike chat client calls).

Q43. How does browser TTS work for Hiro’s questions? Latency / UX issues?
A43. lib/tts.ts uses speechSynthesis to speak question text while the avatar
     shows speaking state. Pros: free, local, zero media server. Cons: voice
     quality varies by OS/browser; latency before utterance; interruptions need
     careful cancel; no perfect lip-sync. UX mitigations: show text on screen,
     allow skip/replay, don’t block answering UI entirely on TTS.

Q44. Why isn’t the AI interview on WebRTC / LiveKit?
A44. Hiro is not a second human — we don’t need peer A/V rooms, SFU, or 2-party
     tokens for Q&A. WebRTC would add infra cost and failure modes. Coaching is
     the LiveKit use case. AI interview = app-driven turn taking + STT/TTS.

Q45. How do you handle browser STT unsupported (Safari / permissions denied)?
A45. Offer text input always; mic test in setup surfaces permission issues early;
     fall back to MediaRecorder + /api/transcribe where possible; clear error
     copy when SpeechRecognition missing. Don’t hard-block the interview on voice
     alone — accessibility and completion rate depend on text path.

Q46. How would you make voice feel more “realtime conversational”?
A46. Partial transcripts with barge-in; stream LLM tokens into TTS (chunked speak);
     server VAD; interruptible Hiro; shorter follow-ups; optional realtime APIs
     (OpenAI Realtime / LiveKit agents) for true duplex. Keep captions. Measure
     time-to-first-audio and interruption success rate.

7. SCORING & REPORTS
================================================================================

Q47. Explain the five score axes and how overall rating maps to hire bands.
A47. Axes: communication, technicalDepth, problemSolving, structure, ownership
     (0–100-ish clamped). overall = average of five.
     Rating thresholds: ≥85 Strong hire; ≥75 Hire; ≥65 Lean hire; else No hire.
     Narrative strengths/improvements derive partly from axis thresholds and tone
     adjustments.

Q48. How does heuristic scoring work before AI enrichment?
A48. Baseline ~62 + signals: average word counts, structure keywords (e.g. result,
     because, trade-off style hits), communication tone adjustment from responses,
     difficulty adjustment (hard −5 / easy +4), small jitter. This yields numeric
     breakdown even without OpenAI. Then generateReportWithAI can enrich text fields.

Q49. Does AI rewrite only narrative fields or also numeric scores? Is that consistent?
A49. Primary numeric scores come from heuristics; AI enrichment focuses on
     strengths, improvements, nextSteps, jobReadiness, detailedAnalysis. That can
     feel inconsistent if prose implies a different bar than numbers — a known
     MVP tension. Production fix: either AI-calibrated scores with rubrics or
     clearly label heuristic scores vs AI commentary.

Q50. How are reports persisted (interview_reports + JSONB)?
A50. Server APIs under /api/reports accept/store reports keyed by user; schema has
     interview_reports with score columns plus report_data JSONB for the full
     structured report (answers, analysis, etc.). Listing/progress endpoints read
     from DB. Client may still cache in localStorage for UX, but Postgres is source
     of truth for durable history.

Q51. How does PDF generation work? Emailing the report?
A51. Client/lib uses jspdf to render a downloadable PDF from report fields.
     /api/reports/[id]/email sends a copy via Nodemailer/SMTP. Separation: PDF is
     user-driven export; email is server notification with auth checks on ownership.

Q52. How does progress across multiple interviews get computed?
A52. /api/reports/progress (and related client views) aggregates prior reports —
     score trends, weak areas, attempt counts (e.g. usedAttemptsThisMonth helper).
     Prior context feeds the next interview config so Hiro targets weaknesses and
     avoids repeated questions.

Q53. How would you stop a user from faking high scores before POST /api/reports?
A53. Today risk is real because scoring can run client-side. Fix: stream answers to
     server each turn; server recomputes scores; sign report payloads; reject
     client-supplied breakdowns; rate-limit; compare transcript length/timing
     heuristics; optionally re-grade async with AI on server. Treat client scores
     as untrusted display-only until verified.

8. PAYMENTS (RAZORPAY)
================================================================================

Q54. Walk through create order → Checkout.js → verify signature.
A54. Authenticated user hits /api/payments/razorpay/order → server creates Razorpay
     order + payment_transactions row (productType interview|coaching).
     Client opens Razorpay Checkout.js with order id + public key.
     On success, client sends orderId, paymentId, signature to /api/payments/
     razorpay/verify → HMAC verify → mark paid / unlock access / advance booking.
     Webhook is a parallel source of truth for async confirmation.

Q55. How do you verify Razorpay signatures safely (timingSafeEqual)?
A55. Payment: HMAC-SHA256 of `orderId|paymentId` with key secret, compare to
     signature via crypto.timingSafeEqual on buffers (constant-time; avoids timing
     attacks from naive string compare). Webhooks: HMAC of raw body with webhook
     secret, same timingSafeEqual. Length mismatches must be handled carefully
     (equal-length buffers) to avoid throws.

Q56. Interview vs coaching: how does productType branch the flow?
A56. payment_transactions.product_type is interview | coaching. Order/verify
     handlers branch: interview unlocks AI interview access / plan flags; coaching
     attaches to a booking/slot-hold and moves booking toward pending approval.
     Same Razorpay rails, different domain side effects after paid.

Q57. How do webhooks + razorpay_webhook_events give idempotency?
A57. Webhook handler verifies signature, logs event id/payload in
     razorpay_webhook_events, and skips re-processing duplicates. Prevents double
     unlock / double booking confirmation when Razorpay retries. Verify endpoint
     and webhook should converge on the same paid state machine.

Q58. What if verify succeeds but webhook never arrives (or vice versa)?
A58. Design for either path to finalize payment safely (idempotent state
     transitions). If verify paid first, webhook no-ops. If webhook first, verify
     sees already paid. Ops: reconcile unpaid orders vs Razorpay dashboard; alert
     on verify/webhook signature failures; never unlock without a verified signal.

Q59. How are refunds / refund-requests modeled and audited?
A59. Users can request refunds on coaching bookings; refund_events (and admin
     refunds.manage permission) audit the trail. Admins view/manage via admin
     payments/refunds surfaces. Important: refunds should update booking/payment
     status consistently and remain append-only for audit.

Q60. Where is plan/access unlocked after payment (lib/plan-access.ts)?
A60. INTERVIEW_PRICE_INR = 199 is centralized there; helpers describe premium
     feature flags / attempt counting. Actual unlock is driven by successful
     payment verification updating user/plan or granting access flags, with
     checkout gating the setup route. (Note: some premium helpers currently return
     permissive defaults — know what’s enforced vs aspirational in code reviews.)

9. COACHING & SCHEDULING
================================================================================

Q61. End-to-end: pick coach → slot hold → pay → coach approve → calendar + meeting.
A61. User picks coach/slot on /schedule → create short-lived coaching_slot_holds →
     Razorpay pay (productType coaching) → booking pending → coach receives email
     with approval token → on approve: Google Calendar event + ensure LiveKit room
     / meeting URL → user (and coach) join /meeting/[bookingId] → session/transcript
     → feedback token flow; refunds if needed.

Q62. Why short-lived coaching_slot_holds plus unique (coach, starts_at)?
A62. Hold reserves the slot during checkout so two browsers don’t sell the same
     time. Unique constraint on bookings (coach + starts_at) is the hard DB
     guarantee against double booking. Holds expire so abandoned checkouts release
     inventory. Defense in depth: UX hold + DB uniqueness.

Q63. Race: two users checkout the same slot — who wins?
A63. First to successfully create the hold/booking row under constraints wins; the
     other gets a conflict error from unique index / hold logic. Payment without a
     valid hold should not confirm the booking. Exact winner = first committed
     transaction, not first click.

Q64. What does coach email approval do exactly?
A64. Tokenized GET/approve endpoint validates hashed token, transitions booking to
     approved/scheduled state, creates/updates Google Calendar event, ensures
     LiveKit room and stores meeting URL/room name on the booking, notifies
     parties. Rejects invalid/expired tokens. Keeps coaches out-of-band from
     needing an always-open dashboard for first confirmation.

Q65. How is Google Calendar integrated (service account, Meet, edge cases)?
A65. lib/integrations/google-calendar.ts uses googleapis with a service account
     to create events (and Meet links where configured). Edge cases: attendee
     permissions with service accounts, domain-wide delegation, timezone on
     starts_at, failed calendar still allowing meeting URL via LiveKit, retries/
     idempotency if approve is double-clicked.

Q66. How does coaching feedback via token work?
A66. After session, a one-time feedback token lets the user submit ratings without
     full login friction (or in addition to logged-in flow). Stored in
     coaching_feedback 1:1 with booking. Cron reminders nudge incomplete feedback.
     Tokens hashed at rest like other email tokens.

Q67. How would you handle timezone differences for slots?
A67. Store all slot instants in UTC (timestamptz); render in user/coach timezone
     from user_settings or browser. Availability JSON should be interpreted with
     an explicit TZ. Calendar events must include correct TZ to avoid off-by-one
     hour bugs. Never schedule on naive local strings without offset.

10. LIVEKIT MEETINGS
================================================================================

Q68. How are rooms created and tokens issued?
A68. lib/meeting/service ensureRoom creates/reuses a room name per booking via
     LiveKit RoomService (maxParticipants: 2). /api/meeting/token issues a
     server-signed participant access token (livekit-server-sdk) for the
     authenticated authorized user/coach. Client livekit-client joins with URL+token.
     Secrets stay server-side.

Q69. Why max 2 participants?
A69. Coaching is 1:1 (candidate + coach). Cap reduces abuse (extra joins), simplifies
     billing/moderation assumptions, and matches product intent. Observers/admins
     would need an explicit design change (hidden participant roles).

Q70. How do session start/end and recording/transcription chunks work?
A70. session/start and session/end APIs mark meeting lifecycle on the booking.
     Client MeetingExperience can MediaRecorder chunk audio and POST
     transcribe-chunk; transcripts land in meeting_transcripts. End triggers
     cleanup paths; cron also closes stale active meetings. Recording retention
     policies should be explicit for compliance.

Q71. Explain meeting moderation (regex poaching/contact flags) — strengths & limits.
A71. lib/meeting/moderation.ts scans transcript text with regex for poaching,
     contact sharing, policy phrases → meeting_moderation_alerts.
     Strengths: cheap, deterministic, low latency, no extra LLM cost.
     Limits: easy to evade, language-limited, false positives/negatives, no
     context. LLM moderator or classifier would be richer but costlier/slower.

Q72. What does the LiveKit webhook handle?
A72. /api/webhooks/livekit receives room/participant lifecycle events to sync
     server state (e.g. room ended, participants left) and support cleanup /
     auditing. Must verify webhook authenticity and stay idempotent.

Q73. What does the hourly meeting-lifecycle cron clean up, and why?
A73. Finds stale “active” meetings past expected end/grace, marks them ended, and
     tryDeleteLiveKitRoom to free resources and disconnect stragglers. Prevents
     orphan rooms/cost bleed when clients crash without calling session/end.
     Feedback reminder cron is separate (nudge unreviewed sessions).

11. DATABASE & SCHEMA
================================================================================

Q74. Walk through the main tables and relationships.
A74. users — identity, role, plan, status.
     email_verification_tokens / password_reset_tokens — hashed OTPs.
     interview_reports — per-user reports + JSONB payload.
     coaches — profile, rates, availability JSON, tech areas.
     coaching_slot_holds — ephemeral reservations.
     coaching_bookings — slot, payment, approval, calendar, meeting state.
     coaching_feedback — rating per booking.
     payment_transactions — Razorpay rows by productType.
     razorpay_webhook_events / refund_events — payment audit.
     support_tickets, audit_logs, admin_settings, user_settings.
     meeting_transcripts, meeting_moderation_alerts.
     Relationships center on users ↔ reports/bookings/payments; coaches ↔ bookings.

Q75. Why JSONB for report_data and coach availability?
A75. Reports are nested/evolving (answers, analysis objects) — JSONB avoids constant
     migrations for every narrative field while keeping queryable envelope columns
     (scores, role). Availability is structured but coach-specific; JSONB is flexible
     for weekly patterns. Tradeoff: weaker relational constraints inside JSON —
     validate with Zod at write time.

Q76. How are migrations managed (drizzle/, db:migrate)?
A76. SQL migrations live under drizzle/ (0000…0020+). npm run db:migrate runs
     scripts/db-migrate.mjs tracking applied files (e.g. _hiro_migrations).
     Schema source of truth: lib/db/schema.ts. Deploy rule: migrate before
     rolling out code that depends on new columns.

Q77. Indexes / uniqueness you’d highlight in a schema review?
A77. Case-insensitive unique email on users; unique (coach, starts_at) on bookings;
     unique meeting room name; unique/idempotent webhook event ids; indexes on
     foreign keys and common filters (status, product_type, expires_at on holds).
     These enforce money/scheduling correctness under concurrency.

Q78. How would you migrate off hybrid localStorage mid-interview state?
A78. Create interview_sessions table; autosave answers/config server-side each turn;
     resume by session id; keep localStorage as offline cache only; dual-read then
     drop client-only report lists. Enables multi-device resume and anti-tamper
     scoring.

Q79. Soft-delete vs hard-delete for users/bookings — what do you do today?
A79. Suspension is soft status on users (not hard delete by default). Bookings
     cancel/reschedule via status transitions. Admin may have delete permissions
     for some entities — prefer soft-delete/anonymize for payments and transcripts
     due to audit/compliance. Be explicit in answers about what is physically
     deleted vs status-flagged in current code.

Q80. What can admins do vs coaches vs users?
A80. Users: interview, reports, schedule coaching, support tickets, settings.
     Coaches: coach portal, manage/view their bookings/sessions.
     Admins (tiered): users, coaches, sessions/reports, bookings, payments/refunds,
     support, analytics, audit, team invites — gated by permission matrix.
     super_admin ⊂ all capabilities.

Q81. How does the permissions matrix stay shared between API and UI?
A81. Single module lib/auth/permissions.ts exports ROLES, PERMISSIONS, and
     role→permission maps. API requirePermission and AdminShell nav gating both
     import it — prevents UI showing actions the API would 403 (and vice versa).

Q82. What goes into audit_logs?
A82. Sensitive admin actions (user updates, suspensions, role changes, etc.) for
     accountability. Admins with audit.view/export can review. Good practice:
     who/when/what/before-after, never store secrets in audit payloads.

Q83. How do support tickets flow?
A83. Users create tickets via /api/support/tickets (and support UI). Admins with
     support.view/manage handle them in admin support surfaces. Email may notify
     on create/update depending on SMTP wiring. Status lifecycle should be tracked
     on support_tickets rows.

Q84. What admin settings are live-configurable?
A84. admin_settings singleton JSON plus /api/settings/public for safe public flags
     (announcements, feature copy, pricing-related display, etc.). settings.update
     permission protects writes. Keeps ops changes off a full redeploy when
     appropriate.

Q85. How would you invite a team member / sub-admin safely?
A85. team.invite + role.assign.* permissions; invite creates/updates user with
     least privilege (sub_admin by default); email verification required; only
     higher roles can assign admin/super_admin; audit the assignment; avoid
     sharing SUPER_ADMIN_PASSWORD bootstrap. Prefer invite links over shared
     passwords.

Q86. What emails does the system send (verify, reset, coaching, report, support)?
A86. Email verification, password reset, coaching approval/notifications, report

     lib/email + Nodemailer templates/helpers. All sensitive links use hashed

Q87. How is Nodemailer / SMTP configured for local vs prod?
A87. Env-driven SMTP (host/port/user/pass/from) — Gmail/SendGrid/etc. Local can

Q88. How would you make email delivery reliable (queues, retries)?
A88. Push send jobs to a queue (BullMQ/SQS); retry with backoff; dead-letter;

     provider. Today’s sync Nodemailer in request path is MVP — fine until volume
     or latency hurts UX.

Q89. Coaching feedback reminders — who triggers them and when?
A89. Cron route /api/cron/coaching-feedback-reminders (secret-gated), typically
     scheduled hourly like meeting lifecycle. Selects completed bookings missing
     feedback and emails a tokenized reminder. Prevents silent drop-off after
     paid sessions.

================================================================================
14. SECURITY DEEP DIVE
================================================================================

Q90. Top 3 security issues you’d fix before production.
A90. 1) Remove NEXT_PUBLIC_OPENAI_API_KEY / client LLM — server proxy + spend caps.
     2) Server-side authoritative scoring and answer persistence (anti-tamper).
     3) Harden secrets/bootstrap (AUTH_SECRET, SUPER_ADMIN_*, webhook secrets),
        CSRF strategy for state-changing cookie auth, and IDOR audits on
        reports/bookings. Honorable mention: resume prompt-injection defenses.

Q91. Client-side OpenAI key exposure — attack scenarios?
A91. Attacker extracts key from JS bundle → runs unlimited completions → bill
     shock; uses your key for unrelated abuse → provider suspension; scrapes
     prompts/IP. Mitigate: revoke key, move server-side, per-user quotas, monitor
     usage anomalies, separate restricted keys if any client path must remain.

Q92. Client-orchestrated interview scoring — tampering scenarios?
A92. User patches network response or local generateReport output → POST inflated
     scores; skips weak answers; forges report_data. Business impact: fake “Strong
     hire” PDFs, polluted analytics, unfair coaching recommendations. Fix: server
     grade from trusted transcripts only.

Q93. CSRF on cookie auth — how does sameSite: lax help / not help?
A93. sameSite=lax blocks most cross-site POST cookie sends from foreign sites,
     reducing classic CSRF. It does not protect same-site subdomain attacks, or
     all GET side effects (lax sends cookies on top-level GET navigations).
     Dangerous GETs (coach-approve) must be token-gated (they are). For strictness:
     CSRF tokens or sameSite=strict + careful UX; Prefer POST for mutations.

Q94. Are admin APIs protected against IDOR on bookings/reports?
A94. Pattern should be: authenticate → authorize permission → scope query by
     role (admin global vs user ownership vs coach assignment). User report fetch
     by id must verify userId == session.sub. Admin routes use requirePermission.
     Always cite checking ownership in handlers during interviews; call out any
     gaps you’d audit with automated tests.

Q95. How do you sanitize / limit transcript and resume uploads?
A95. Enforce max file size/type (PDF/TXT), max extracted character length, virus
     scanning at higher tiers, store outside public web root if files retained,
     escape when rendering, don’t execute resume content. Transcripts: length
     caps per chunk, moderation pipeline, retention limits. Zod validates API
     shapes; content safety is deeper than schema.

Q96. Secrets in .env — what must never be NEXT_PUBLIC_?
A96. Never public: AUTH_SECRET, DB URL, SMTP passwords, RAZORPAY_KEY_SECRET,
     RAZORPAY_WEBHOOK_SECRET, OPENAI_API_KEY (server), LIVEKIT API secret,
     Google service account private key, CRON_SECRET, SUPER_ADMIN_PASSWORD.
     NEXT_PUBLIC_ is fine only for truly public keys (e.g. Razorpay key id).
     Today’s NEXT_PUBLIC_OPENAI_API_KEY is the anti-pattern to fix.

15. RELIABILITY, OBSERVABILITY & TESTING
================================================================================

Q97. How do you know a payment/webhook failed in production?
A97. Log verify/webhook failures with order ids (not full secrets); alert on
     signature failures and stuck “created” transactions; reconcile Razorpay
     dashboard vs payment_transactions; admin payments UI; dead-letter unmatched
     webhooks. Metrics: payment success rate, verify vs webhook lag.

Q98. What would you log vs never log (PII, tokens)?
A98. Log: request ids, user ids, booking ids, error codes, latency, payment ids.
     Never log: raw passwords, session JWTs, email raw tokens, full card data
     (you shouldn’t have it), OpenAI keys, webhook secrets, full resume/transcript
     by default in info logs. Redact emails if required by policy; hash tokens.

Q99. How would you test the question engine without burning OpenAI credits?
A99. Unit test heuristics (buildInterviewPlan, maybeGenerateFollowUp, scoring
     thresholds, isGenericQuestion) with fixtures; mock getOpenAIClient; golden
     JSON fixtures for AI parsers; contract tests for response_format shapes;
     optional recorded replay of model outputs. CI should not need a real key.

Q100. Critical integration tests you’d write for Razorpay verify + webhook?
A100. Valid signature → paid + unlock; invalid signature → 4xx no unlock;
      duplicate webhook → single side effect; verify then webhook → idempotent;
      webhook then verify → idempotent; wrong productType side effects; suspended
      user blocked; coaching requires hold/booking consistency.

Q101. How do you handle OpenAI latency / timeouts mid-interview?
A101. Timeouts on client calls; fall back to heuristic follow-up/end; show
      non-blocking UI (“thinking”) so TTS/answer capture isn’t frozen forever;
      retry once on transient errors; degrade gracefully without ending the
      session abruptly. Server-side: circuit breaker + queue.

Q102. What’s your rollback plan if a bad migration ships?
A102. Forward-fix migrations preferred; keep expand/contract pattern; backup
      before migrate; if broken, redeploy previous app compatible with DB, then
      write a down/fix migration. Avoid destructive column drops without a
      two-phase release. Document migrate-before-code order.

17. TRADEOFFS & SENIOR JUDGMENT
================================================================================

Q108. Biggest architectural compromise you knowingly made?
A108. Client-side OpenAI + hybrid localStorage for interview orchestration —
      maximized demo speed, accepted key exposure and tamperable scoring. Second:
      monolith doing payments + meetings + AI. Both were intentional MVP bets with
      a clear hardening path.

Q109. Heuristic + AI hybrid — when does that pattern fail?
A109. Fails when heuristics and AI disagree (scores vs prose); when templates feel
      fake after users expect AI depth; when generic-filter blocks too much and
      overuses fallbacks; when offline mode silently trains users on weaker signal.
      Needs monitoring of fallback rates and human eval of question quality.

Q110. Regex moderation vs an LLM moderator — tradeoffs?
A110. Regex: cheap, explainable, fast, brittle. LLM: contextual, multilingual,
      costlier, slower, harder to audit, can hallucinate flags. Practical approach:
      regex for high-precision obvious leaks + async LLM for suspicious sessions;
      human review queue for high-severity alerts.

Q111. If rebuilding today, what would you change first?
A111. Server-side interview engine and scoring from day one; session persistence
      in Postgres; no public OpenAI key; thinner client. Keep Next monolith initially
      but with clear domain modules and queues for AI/email.

Q112. How would you A/B test question quality or scoring fairness?
A112. Assign experiment keys per user/session; variant personas/prompts/scoring
      rubrics; measure completion, follow-up acceptance, coaching conversion,
      human-rated question relevance, score distribution by cohort (fresher vs
      experienced). Avoid shipping unfair score shifts without calibration sets.

Q113. Legal/compliance: storing transcripts, recordings, resumes — concerns?
A113. PII/sensitive data: resume contact info, voice biometrics-ish audio,
      meeting recordings. Need retention limits, deletion/export requests, purpose
      limitation, secure storage, coach/candidate consent, India DPDP / policy
      alignment, subprocessors (OpenAI, LiveKit, Razorpay) in privacy policy.
      Don’t log raw transcripts broadly.

Q114. Cost control: how do you cap OpenAI spend per user/session?
A114. Server proxy with per-user daily quotas; max tokens; max follow-ups already
      capped by difficulty; cache plans; use mini models; hard budget alarms;
      deny client direct key. Track cost per completed interview as a unit economic.
